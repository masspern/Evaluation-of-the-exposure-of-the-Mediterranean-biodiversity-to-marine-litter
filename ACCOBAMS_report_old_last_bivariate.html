0.1 Introduction

The ACCOBAMS data for PlasticBusters MPAs Consortium used in this analysis are related to observations of marine mammals, sea-turtles and marine litter within the survey blocks n.04, 08a, 08b,09, 10, 11, 12, 13, 20, 21 and 22b during the ASI aerial survey carried out in 2018 and 2019; these specific datasets span from June to August 2018. The analysis on ACCOBAMS data was conducted following https://distancesampling.org.

The following picture show the workflfow of Density Surface Modelling

Figure 1: Workflfow of Density Surface Modelling

0.2 Input data

Input data are aerial surveys of mediterranean areas (Strata) in shapefile, access database and excel format. Data of single strata have been merged, in orde to obtain a single complete dataset. These data contain transects with detailed description of meteo-marine conditions occurred, as well as specific information about sightings in term of group, family, species observed, pods, observer etc.
At a first look at the sightings see that mostly they consist in plastic trash or unidentified trash.




Figure 2: Observations (red dots) in ACCOBAMS data


1 Model zones

The data are distributed in specific zones of Mediterranean area. These zones have been analised separately, since they are quite split from one another.

The sampling areas considered are:

  • NW Mediterranean
  • Sicily channel
  • Ionian-Aegean sea

The abundance estimation was developed on EEA reference grid (https://www.eea.europa.eu/data-and-maps/data/eea-reference-grids-2). This is a Pan-European grid system covering EU member states as well as EEA cooperating countries and neighbouring countries to the East. It is based on the recommendation at the 1st European Workshop on Reference Grids in 2003 and later INSPIRE geographical grid systems.

Figure 3: Studied areas.

1.1 Observation and segment data

All of the data for this analysis has been pre-formatted according to dsm R package.

4 datasets have been prepared starting from ACCOBAMS data:

  • segdata ==> The analysis of distance sampling usually start from chopping transects into smaller pieces.
  • distdata ==> A similar dataset that holds the distance sampling data that will be used to fit the detection function.
  • obsdata ==> links the distance data to the segments.
  • preddata ==> this is the only spatial dataset that was not present in input data; It’s been created dividing the different zones into grids (10x10 km) which will be used to perform the abundancy evaluation.

2 Exploratory data analysis

2.1 Distance data

Distance Sampling is a process in which a study area is surveyed in order to estimate the size of the population within it. It can be thought of as an extension to plot sampling. However, while plot sampling assumes that all objects within the plots are detected, distance sampling relaxes this assumption. For further information see https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/DSsim/vignettes/Investigating_Covariates_and_Truncation.html

The firt step in analysing distance sampled data is to check the perpendicular distances from each detected object to the transect it was detected from and fit a detection function. From this function we can estimate how many objects were missed and hence the total number in the covered area.

The top panels below, show histograms of observed distances and cluster size, while the other panels show the relationship between observed distance and Beaufort sea state.

3 Fitting a DSM

3.1 Tweedie distribution

The tweedie distribution is a new comer to generalized linear model framework. It has three parameters, μ, σ2, ρ, and has a number of unique characteristics. It is a special case of exponential dispersion models, but is a generalization of several familiar probability distributions, including the normal, gamma, inverse Gaussian and Poisson distributions.

Distributions ρ = 0 gaussian ρ = 1 poisson ρ = 2 gamma ρ = 3 inverse gaussian

The probability density function cannot be evaluated directly. Instead special algorithms must be created to calculate the density. Interested statisticians should read Evaluation of Tweedie exponential dispersion model densities by Fourier inversion. The author created the R package tweedie; which GlmSimulatoR relies upon.

references:

  • Wikipedia. Tweedie distribution.
  • Jørgensen, B. (1987). Exponential dispersion models.
  • Tweedie, M. C. K. (1984). An index with distinguishes between some important exponential families.

By setting group=TRUE, the abundance of clusters/groups rather than individuals can be estimated (though we ignore this here). Note we set method="REML" to ensure that smooth terms are estimated reliably.

5.3 Model selection

Assuming that models have “passed” the Autocorrelation and are sufficiently flexible, we may be left with a choice of which model is “best”. There are several methods for choosing the best model – AIC, REML/GCV scores, deviance explained, full cross-validation with test data and so on.

It is possible to create a results table to compare the various models that have been fitted so far in terms of their abundance estimates and associated uncertainties.

6 Variance estimation

Obviously point estimates of abundance are important, but we should also calculate uncertainty around these abundance estimates. DSM package functions to perform these calculations and display the resulting uncertainty estimates are shown here.

We can use the approach of Williams et al (2011), which allows us to incorporate both detection function uncertainty and spatial model (GAM) uncertainty in our estimates of the variance.

There are two approaches to estimating the uncertainty of an abundance estimate in dsm.

  • dsm.var.gam which assumes that the spatial model and detection function parts of the model are independent of each other. In this case the squared coefficients of variation for each model component are added.
  • dsm.var.prop which takes into account the fact that detection probability may be correlated with the spatial part of the model. It uses methods described in Bravington, Miller and Hedley, 2019.

dsm.var.prop can only be applied when there is a covariate in the detection function that varies at the level of the segments, and is recorded at each segment (for example Beaufort). We don’t have that situation here, so we opt for dsm.var.gam.

Both methods estimate the variance of the abundance for each element in the list provided in pred.data. In our case we wish to obtain an abundance for each of the prediction cells, so we use split to chop our data set into list elements to give to dsm.var.gam (or dsm.var.prop).

dsm_var_ind <- dsm.var.gam(dsm.xy, preddata, preddata$Area)
summary(dsm_var_ind)

7 Bivariate analysis

This analysis was conducted through a LISA (Local Indicators of Spatial Association) bivariate approach (Anselin, 1995), considering first the relative spatial distribution between marine mammals and plastic trash, and then between turtles and plastic trash. This approach allows us to evaluate the different clusters starting from identification of a threshold for significant values (in this case 95° percentile confidence interval has been chosen). Besides, the threshold to cluster the statistically high/low presence of targets, according to a precautionary choice , was set at 1.5 standard deviation value of their probability of occurrence (i.e. around 68° percentile).

Plastic Busters MPAs Project focused on applying Marine litter prevention and mitigation actions in 10 Mediterranean MPAs. In the following maps it is possible to evaluate the overlapping of the clusters of relative distributions of marine mammals vs plastic trash, with some of these MPAs (namely the National Marine Park of Zakynthos, the Cabrera Archipelago, the Tuscan Archipelago and the Pelagos Sanctuary which covers a very large extent (about 90.000 km2).)

8 Further developments

This work may be considered as a first step in analysing ACCOBAMS data; important improvemets may derive from:

  • Considering covariates in the analysis (e.g. depth, distance from the coast, distance from river mouths, sea state both at the moment of survey and as climatological data, bio-geochemical parameters of water, sighting conditions by the observers in terms of turbidity, precipitations, etc.)
  • Stratification of estimates of encouters by year, region and season.
  • Analysis of other species/objects observed (apart from the three types selected).
  • Analysis of vessel-based survey and integrate them into the present analysis.
  • Testing other kind of models both for detectability and for density abundance estimation. In particular it could be interesting to check the results obtained using the BIOMOD2 platform (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/308177094_biomod2_Ensemble_platform_for_species_distribution_modelling).
